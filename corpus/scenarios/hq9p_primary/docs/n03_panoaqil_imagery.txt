IMAGERY ANALYSIS SUMMARY
Ref: IA-2025-0411-PK-AD-SIND
Subject: Two separate long-range SAM emplacements, Pano Aqil Cantonment and environs, Sukkur District,
         Sindh, Pakistan; plus one occupied dispersal site, Ghotki District
Imagery basis: commercial EO, three passes (11 Mar 2025, 2025-03-19, 27/03/2025); one archival frame
         (Nov 2023) pulled for change detection

1. WHY THIS REPORT EXISTS

Routine survey tasking over northern Sindh returned two spatially separate prepared emplacements, both
carrying signatures consistent with a Chinese-origin long-range surface-to-air system of the HQ-9 family.
They are not one position imaged twice. They are 5.6 km apart, they differ in layout and in at least one
major vehicle type, and they were both occupied on all three passes. This desk is reporting them as two
emplacements because that is what the imagery supports, and is deliberately NOT reporting how many units
they represent, because the imagery does not support that at all. Section 5 states that limitation in
full; readers taking anything from this report should take Section 5 with it.

3. OBSERVED SIGNATURES

NORTH EMPLACEMENT
  - Six elongated canister-type objects in a fan around a central hardstand, spacing and dimensions
    consistent with published HQ-9-family transporter-erector-launcher geometry.
  - One large octagonal-planform radar-type object on a separate prepared hardstand approximately 340 m
    east of the launcher fan. Planform and mount geometry are consistent with the HT-233-type engagement
    radar associated with this family. Type identification is by planform comparison only and is not a
    positive identification.
  - Two soft-skin utility vehicles, one probable command/communications shelter, generator trailers.
  - Occupied on all three passes. Object count stable at six across all three.

SOUTH-EAST EMPLACEMENT
  - Four elongated canister-type objects in a shallower arc, same object dimensions as at the north
    emplacement within measurement error.
  - One radar-type object of a VISIBLY DIFFERENT planform: rectangular rather than octagonal, on a lower
    mount, sited approximately 210 m north of the launcher arc. It is not the same class of object as the
    radar at the north emplacement. This desk is not naming it; it is noted in some open literature that
    Pakistani long-range air defence holdings include more than one engagement/acquisition radar fit, and
    a rectangular planform is consistent with several of them.
  - One additional large vehicle, function not determined, present on the 11 Mar pass and absent on the
    other two.
  - Occupied on all three passes. Object count stable at four across all three.

DISPERSAL SITE, GHOTKI
  - Four canister-type objects, one rectangular-planform radar-type object, two soft-skin vehicles.
  - Occupied on all three passes. Not present on the Nov 2023 archival frame, on which the pad is graded
    but empty — so the occupancy post-dates that frame; the imagery cannot date it more closely than that.

4. CHANGE DETECTION AGAINST THE ARCHIVAL FRAME

On the Nov 2023 frame the north emplacement is present and occupied with the same six-object fan. The
south-east emplacement is present as prepared, graded ground with revetments cut, but is EMPTY. The
dispersal site is graded and empty as noted above. The reasonable readings of that are that the south-east
emplacement was occupied at some point between Nov 2023 and Mar 2025 by equipment that came from
somewhere, and this desk cannot say from where. It has not been imaged in the interval.

5. WHAT THIS REPORT DOES NOT ESTABLISH — READ THIS BEFORE CITING ANYTHING ABOVE

This report names no formation. Overhead imagery shows equipment at a place; it does not show whose
equipment it is, and this desk has no basis to attach any unit identity to either emplacement or to the
dispersal site. Nothing in Section 3 should be quoted as a unit location.

More importantly, the report does NOT establish how many units are represented. Two occupied emplacements
5.6 km apart, both under one cantonment, are consistent with at least three readings this desk cannot
choose between:

  (a) two separate units, one at each emplacement;
  (b) one unit holding two emplacements, which is normal practice for a formation of more than one
      battery and would be entirely unremarkable;
  (c) one unit at one emplacement and equipment of a training, conversion or depot establishment at the
      other.

The differing radar planform is a real observed difference and it is the best discriminator in this
collection, but it does not settle the question either: one unit can hold two radar fits during a
conversion, and two units can share one fit. Anyone wanting a unit count for Pano Aqil needs a source
this desk does not have — an order-of-battle reference, a formation-level statement, or an equipment
identifier that ties a specific vehicle to a specific unit. Absent that, the count at this cantonment is
open, and should be recorded as open rather than as one or as two.

The same caution applies with more force to the Ghotki dispersal site. That it is occupied by equipment of
the same general class as the two cantonment emplacements is not evidence that it came from either of
them, and it is certainly not evidence that either emplacement has been vacated — both were occupied on
every pass in this collection, including the passes on which the dispersal site was occupied. A reading
that treats the dispersal occupancy as a move away from the cantonment is contradicted by this collection's
own frames.
