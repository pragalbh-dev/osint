CORPS COMMANDER VISITS AIR DEFENCE FORMATION AT PANO AQIL CANTONMENT

Pano Aqil – 18 Feb 2025: The Corps Commander today visited 22 Air Defence Regiment, also known as the
Indus Gunners, at its garrison at Pano Aqil Cantonment, Sukkur District, where he reviewed the operational
readiness of the formation following its re-equipment with a long-range air defence weapon system.

On arrival the Corps Commander was received by the Commander 3 Air Defence Brigade and by the Commanding
Officer of 22 Air Defence Regiment (hereinafter referred to as the Regiment). He was given a briefing on
the Regiment's air defence task in the northern Sindh sector, on its integration into the wider air defence
architecture, and on the training state achieved since re-equipment.

The Regiment was raised in the medium-range air defence role and has, reportedly following the completion
of conversion training last year, taken the long-range system onto its inventory. The Corps Commander was
shown a static display of the system's launcher and radar vehicles and was briefed on the crew training
cycle. In line with standing practice, no details of the number of batteries, launchers or missile rounds
held by the Regiment were disclosed, and no such figures should be inferred from the display.

Addressing officers and men, the Corps Commander said that the air defence of the country's southern
approaches was a task of the highest importance and that the professional standard achieved by the
Regiment in a short conversion period reflected credit on all ranks. He noted that one of the Regiment's
batteries is presently deployed forward on operational tasking away from the cantonment and conveyed his
appreciation to that battery through the Commanding Officer, observing that the Regiment is at present
holding both its garrison responsibilities and a forward commitment at the same time.

The Corps Commander later planted a sapling at the Regiment's lines and signed the visitors' book before
departing. He was accompanied by the Commander 3 Air Defence Brigade throughout the visit.

The Pakistan Army's Corps of Army Air Defence continues to modernise its holdings in line with the
evolving aerial threat. Further conversions are planned in a phased manner; no timeline has been announced.
