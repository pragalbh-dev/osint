PAKISTAN MILITARY VEHICLE REGISTER — WORKING COMPILATION
Air Defence section · heavy transporter and launcher vehicles
Compiled from contributor photographs. Rolling document, last edit 02 April 2025.

HOW THIS LIST WORKS. Every entry is backed by at least one photograph held in the group archive; the
archive reference is given. Where two contributors read a plate differently the entry records both readings
and, if the disagreement is settled, how. Nothing is listed on the strength of a description alone. We do
not list a vehicle against a unit unless a source we can point at puts it there, and for air defence
vehicles that is almost never — see the note at the foot of this section, which is the single most
important thing on the page and is routinely ignored by people quoting us.

PLATE FORMAT NOTE. Pakistan Army heavy vehicles in this arm carry a four-digit number followed by a
two-letter arm code, stencilled in white on the cab door and repeated smaller on the rear. We read the "AD"
code as Air Defence. That reading is our own inference from where these vehicles turn up and is not
confirmed by any published scheme; treat the letters as an observed marking, not as a decoded one.

COMMENTARY (contributor thread, lightly edited)

The 22 March road move is the interesting one. Open order-of-battle references place two long-range air
defence regiments at Pano Aqil Cantonment — 22 Air Defence Regiment and 47 Air Defence Regiment — and a
launcher photographed at the cantonment in February turning up on the Ghotki road in March is the first
time we have had the same vehicle in two places. What it tells us is that this launcher moved. What people
immediately started saying it tells us is that the regiment moved, and that does not follow from anything
here: one launcher is not a regiment, we do not know which of the two regiments this vehicle belongs to,
and we have no photograph of the cantonment emplacements on or after 22 March to say whether anything was
left behind. Someone with satellite access can answer that and we cannot.

We have been asked several times which regiment 8477 AD belongs to — most of the asking assumes 22 AD Regt
because that is the one people have heard of. We do not know. The vehicle carries no
unit marking we can read, the February display was not captioned by unit in any material we hold, and both
regiments are at the same cantonment. Anyone telling you which one it is, on the evidence in this register,
is telling you something this register does not contain.

NOTE ON UNIT ATTRIBUTION (the bit people skip)

A vehicle register can tell you that a specific vehicle exists, what it looks like, and where and when it
was photographed. Two sightings of one plate is good evidence that one vehicle was in two places. It is not
evidence about formations. Air defence vehicles in this country are not photographed with unit markings, so
every unit attribution you see attached to a plate in a secondary compilation was inferred by somebody from
location, and location is exactly what is ambiguous when two regiments share a cantonment. We would rather
publish a vehicle with no unit against it for years than guess once.
