COUNTING WHAT YOU CAN SEE
An independent re-derivation of the Pakistani long-range SAM holding

Occasional Paper 2025/06 · South Asia Air and Missile Defence Programme
Published 12 May 2025. Supersedes the figure quoted in our own 2023 backgrounder, which is withdrawn.

SUMMARY

The figure "approximately 4 battalions" is quoted widely for Pakistan's long-range surface-to-air holding.
This paper traces where that figure is published, and reports a separate count derived from imagery and
arrived at without reference to it. Our result is that the holding is not fewer than three fire units. We
do not adopt the published figure.

1. WHERE THE PUBLISHED FIGURE IS CARRIED

We identified three outlets carrying "approximately 4 battalions" for the Pakistani holding: a
Jane's-adjacent aggregator, a regional trade blog, and a Quetta-based English-language defence site.

All three attribute the figure to the SIPRI Arms Transfers Database. None of the three reports imagery, an
interview, a document or a count of its own. Two of the three reproduce the surrounding sentence
near-verbatim from the earliest of them; the wording of the third differs but the figure and the
attribution are the same.

Two properties of the SIPRI register are relevant and are not reproduced in any of the three. SIPRI
describes its delivery figures as estimates. And the register records what was TRANSFERRED, which is a
different quantity from what is FIELDED: transferred equipment can sit in training establishments, in
depot, written off, or in reserve, and a register of transfers does not separate those from equipment on
an operational fire unit. Our own 2023 backgrounder repeated the figure with neither property attached,
which is why it is withdrawn here.

2. WHAT WE COUNTED, AND HOW

We reviewed archived commercial electro-optical imagery over Pakistan for the period 2019 to March 2025,
tasked and untasked, against a published signature description for the HQ-9 family. Our unit of counting
was the PREPARED POSITION: a graded, revetted position whose layout matches the reference signature. The
method, the frame list and the per-position tables are in the appendix, which is not distributed with the
public version of this paper.

We identified six prepared positions of the relevant signature type. Of those six, three showed PERSISTENT
occupancy — equipment present on every usable frame across at least four passes spanning at least six
months. Two showed INTERMITTENT occupancy: equipment present on some frames and absent on others, with no
pattern we could resolve against exercise or maintenance cycles we had no access to. One was prepared and
never observed occupied on any frame we hold.

3. POSITIONS AND UNITS

A prepared position is a place; a fire unit is a formation. One fire unit can hold more than one prepared
position — a home position and one or more dispersal or alternate positions is standard practice, and a
unit rotating between them presents as two positions with intermittent occupancy, which is the pattern two
of our six show. One station can also host more than one unit, in which case two positions are two units.

The case in our set where this bites hardest is the cantonment at Pano Aqil in northern Sindh. Two
prepared positions about five and a half kilometres apart were both occupied on every pass in the most
recent collection. Order-of-battle references place two long-range air defence regiments at that
cantonment, and separately record an air defence battery there that they are unable to allocate to either
regiment. No source available to us attaches any position at that cantonment to any named formation. The
two positions differ in one observable — the engagement radar planform is not the same at both — and a
unit converting onto a new fit will also show two planforms. We record the number of units at that
cantonment as OPEN.

4. RESULT

Not fewer than three fire units, on the strength of three persistently occupied positions. We state no
upper bound: the three remaining positions cannot be allocated between additional units, alternate
positions of the first three, and training or depot holdings, and our imagery coverage of Pakistan is not
complete.
